# Broad Daylight — treatment

Impasto Oil Painting · 1080×1920 vertical · 24 fps · ≈ 56 s · English voice-over (Kokoro `am_onyx`, low and calm), burned-in subtitles.
Topic: the Dadeland Mall shooting, Miami, 11 July 1979: the real history behind the game's "Vice City". The narration is the user's, read word for word.
Palette from the brief: dark, moody, muted planes (umber, slate, dusty ochre, grey-green) with **neon pink (#ff3d8b) and teal (#22d3c5)** as the only pure chroma.
No game footage, no game logos, no faces: figures are knife-stroke silhouettes, seen from behind or far away. No real mall or shop branding: the shop sign just says LIQUORS, and the newspaper is a made-up paper.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Straight chronicle.** One plate per sentence: lot → van → door → glass → paper → skyline. Clear, but it reads like a slideshow.
2. **Same wide, repeated.** One locked wide of the storefront plays three times (calm noon → the attack → empty, glass on the ground). It's strong, but it can't hold the newspaper or the skyline the brief asks for.
3. **From noon to neon (chosen).** The film moves from a bleached, muted noon to a city of neon. In the daytime there is **one coloured thing**: the pink LIQUORS sign. It is the first pure colour we see, and the gunfire shatters it. Its pink and teal come back as the newspaper's spot colour, then take over the whole skyline: the colour of "Vice City" grew out of that afternoon. The storefront wide still repeats (before / after) inside the chronicle.

Why 3: it keeps the scenes in the brief's order, and it gives colour a story. The neon accents are not decoration: they are the thread from 1979 to the game's city.

## 2. Logline and arc

A sunny afternoon at a Miami mall turns into the shooting that named the "Cocaine Cowboys", and the neon city that grew from that day.
Setup: noon, heat, ordinary shoppers, one pink sign → Turn: the van, two men walk in, silence, gunfire, glass → Ending: the headline, then the neon skyline; the light goes out.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Learn from* the opening titles of 1980s Miami crime television: pastel and neon against night, slow, confident pushes, synth pads under silence. Also from Edward Hopper's storefronts: hard noon light, empty pavement, dread through stillness.
- *Don't take*: any title design, logo, melody, character or shot. No GTA imagery, type or colours copied from its marketing.

At least two silences: 23.8 (hard zero before the gunfire) and 55.2 (the light goes out). Sound transitions: the siren J-cuts in under the grey wide before we see anything new; the press thump carries over the cut to the skyline as an L-cut.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles: plain serif (Cormorant Garamond, OFL), crisp DOM-style text drawn over a calm dark plane low in the frame (y ≈ 1500–1650 of 1920), ≤ 2 lines, white with a soft shadow; held max(1.8 s, line + 0.6 s). They stay clear of the action, which sits in the upper two-thirds.
- Titles: JULY 11, 1979 and COCAINE COWBOYS are **laid in with the knife** (strokes confined to the letter shapes, an `appear` sweep of about 1 s), in Cormorant Garamond Bold as the reference shape. The newspaper masthead (fictional, "THE EVENING COURIER", with the date line "THURSDAY, JULY 12, 1979") is also painted; body columns are grey knife bars (unreadable on purpose).
- No end card: the film ends on black after the light goes out.
