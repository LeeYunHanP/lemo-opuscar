# The Real Stories Behind GTA 6 — treatment (long form)

Liminal Found Footage · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · 8 min 15 s · English voice-over (Kokoro `am_onyx`), closed captions · no score.
The approved script, with every claim's source, is SCRIPT.md. This note covers how the style carries it.

- **Structure:** an intro, four chapters introduced by camcorder blue-screen cards (Cocaine Cowboys, The Boatlift, The Swamp, The End of the Road), and an outro that returns to the opening tape's framing (a night causeway) until the battery dies.
- **The tapes are the device.** Each scene is one handheld tape; every cut is a tracking tear. Events after camcorders existed record with REC and a dated OSD; events before (1979–1982) play back as tape transfers, with PLAY and a counter instead of a date. The OSD's dates carry the chronology.
- **The 4:3 picture is pillarboxed** in the 16:9 frame, as the style intends; captions are CEA-608 style inside it. Printed matter the viewer must read (the cargo notice) is framed high, clear of the captions.
- **Restraint:** violence is kept far away (a shop front across a parking lot, smoke over rooftops, a dive behind a horizon). No faces, no victims, no real logos or publications.
- **Sound:** only what each tape would have recorded (engines, surf, fluorescent hum, a PA chime, a warning chime, a distant siren), tape noise on every splice, and two digital silences: before the crash and at the end.
- Benchmark (grammar only): 1990s home video and local-news B-roll; essay films that let a calm voice carry what the images refuse to show.
