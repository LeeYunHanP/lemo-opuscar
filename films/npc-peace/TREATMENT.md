The user's beats, in order: a peaceful sunny beach town (dog walker, ice-cream seller, someone waving) with the line "GTA 6 NPCs enjoying their last days of peace 😇" → hard cut to black, "day 1:" → the same town in chaos (a sprinter, the cart on fire, everyone running and screaming) → the dog calmly eats the ice cream → loop.
No game footage, logos or faces: stick figures have no faces by the style's own rule.

**How the style meets the brief.** Paper, ink and one accent colour stay. The town (sea line, sun, palms, beach huts) is drawn as thin grey line art on the paper. The dog and the cart are drawn with the same even-hose line as the people: a stick dog, a line-drawn cart. **The accent (red-orange) means "chaos"**: in the peaceful half nothing is accented; in "day 1" the sprinter has the solid accent head the style gives to "the source of a change", and the fire is the only other accented thing. One meaning, used twice.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Before/after on one locked frame (chosen).** The same wide, same figures, same places: peace, black card, chaos. The joke works only if the viewer sees it is exactly the same town.
2. **The ripple.** One sprinter enters, and a pulse runs along the ground line, flipping each person from calm to panic as it reaches them. Very native to the style, but it delays the chaos, and the brief wants chaos at once after "day 1:".
3. **Pull-back reveal.** Open close on the dog; pull back to the peaceful town; after the card, pull back from the dog again onto chaos. A nice bookend, but it spends the first seconds of a 12 s loop on one character.

Chosen: 1, borrowing the ripple's best part from 2. On the hard cut back to the town, the sprinter is already running, and each figure flips to panic as he passes them (a pulse along the ground line, 0.15 s apart). From 3 it keeps the ending on the dog: a push-in that ends on the dog in the exact screen spot where it walks at t = 0, so the loop is a match cut.

## 2. Logline and arc

A sleepy beach town of stick people is about to become a video-game sandbox. Setup: peace, waves, a wave hello → Turn: black, "day 1:", the same town on fire → Ending: amid the panic, the dog gets the ice cream. The loop sends us back to the calm.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Learn from* four-panel newspaper gag strips (setup, beat, punchline, a last tiny reaction panel), and from silent-film comedy timing (a held calm, an abrupt cut, the deadpan character in the chaos).
- *Don't take*: characters, drawings or jokes from any strip or film, nor anything from the game.

## 6. Cue map

120 BPM (beat 0.5 s), 4/4. Peace: C major, marimba melody + pizzicato bass + shaker + glockenspiel sparkles, bars 1–2.5 (0–5 s). Card: silence + one timpani-like soft hit at 5.0. Chaos (6.0–10.6): C minor, same tune on xylophone in double time, toms on every eighth, pizz bass running eighths, harp gliss at 6.8 (the fire). Last panel (10.6–12.0): marimba alone, three kalimba notes on the bites, the last beat as a pickup into bar 1, so the audio loops with no seam.
