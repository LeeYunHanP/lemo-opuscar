# The Swamp — treatment

Liminal Found Footage · 1080×1920 vertical · 24 fps · ≈ 60 s · English voice-over (Kokoro `am_onyx`, low and calm), closed captions.
Topic: ValuJet Flight 592, 11 May 1996: the Everglades, the real swamp behind the game's map. The narration is the user's, read word for word.
No game footage, no logos, no people, no faces. The jet is a plain, unmarked 1990s twin-jet silhouette. Nothing in the film claims to be real footage: it is drawn, and the tapes are a device.

**How the style meets the brief.** The style is one camcorder tape of empty spaces, told by the camera's own machinery, with no score. The brief's scenes are exteriors, so the film becomes **a stack of home-video tapes spliced together**. Each cut is a tape event (a tracking tear), and the **on-screen display's date and time is the plot**: a sightseeing flight weeks before; a terminal window at 2:04 PM on the day; a hangar weeks later where investigators fill an empty cockpit with test smoke; a distant shore at 2:13 PM; the search the next day; the evidence. The narrator is a voice-over, captioned CEA-608 style. The 4:3 tape sits full-width in the vertical frame (1080×810), with captions in the black below it, as a TV would show them.
The one saturated colour is **the red master-warning lamp** in the cockpit. The fuel sheen on the water is iridescent but muted.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One witness tape.** A single continuous tape from a fishing boat, watching the sky, then the search. Most faithful to "one continuous take", but it cannot show the takeoff, the cockpit or the cause.
2. **The checklist.** An inspector reads a cargo checklist aloud, item by item, intercut with what each item cost. Strong, but it puts the cause first and loses the hook.
3. **Tapes in date order (chosen).** Spliced tapes whose OSD dates walk the viewer from a calm tourist flight over the swamp to the day itself and the day after, then to the paperwork. The swamp bookends it: the last tape repeats the first tape's framing at dusk, and the battery dies.

Why 3: it follows the narration's order, and every cut is a native tape event. "The OSD is plot" carries the dates the narrator speaks.

## 2. Logline and arc

A swamp you only know from above swallowed a jet, and left almost nothing to film.
Setup: the endless sawgrass from a small plane → Turn: the jet climbs out; smoke; silence; a distant dive → Ending: an empty swamp, a sheen of fuel, a form that should have prevented it. The same sawgrass at dusk; the battery dies.

## 3. Benchmark

- *Learn from* 1990s home-video and local-news B-roll (zoom hunting, auto-exposure pumping, date stamps, the sound of the camcorder itself), and from Chris Marker's essay films (a calm voice over images that refuse to show the event).
- *Don't take*: any real footage, news graphics, airline livery or logos; no imagery of victims.

## 6. Cue map (no score)

No music, by the style's rule. The rhythm is the tape: each tear lands on a line's end. The only in-world "music" is a two-note terminal PA chime at 9.3 and the master-warning chime in the cockpit (1 Hz).

## 8. Titles and captions

- Camcorder title generator over shot 1: "EVERGLADES", in the OSD bitmap font, larger.
- OSD in every tape: REC dot, battery, date and time.
- Captions: CEA-608 style, IBM Plex Mono 600, white on black row boxes, ≤ 32 characters a row, centred under the tape. `[SILENCE]` captioned at 26.6. Held ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
- The cargo notice (original wording, no real company names) is part of the picture, held ≥ 4 s uncovered.
